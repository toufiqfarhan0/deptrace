Ivy (People Ops): Congrats, Tara. Welcome. Short answer: placement of a foster child is usually a qualifying life event. Carriers often accept official placement paperwork from the county or the social worker.

Ben (Benefits): @Tara often the placement letter + a photo ID for the child (passport or state ID if available) and a date-of-placement are sufficient. If the letter omits DOB we may ask for an alternate doc that shows DOB (school record, immunization record). Carriers vary.

Tara Nguyen: Child doesn't have a SSN yet. Do I have to wait?

Ivy (People Ops): You can submit a QLE without an SSN in many cases. We'll enroll provisionally and update SSN later once you have it. Note: some carriers request SSN within 30-60 days of enrollment. We'll flag the case as SSN pending.

Ben (Benefits): Please include the social worker's contact and the county placement letter in your submission. If the carrier asks for additional verification (like a court order or foster care agency confirmation), we'll follow up.

Tara Nguyen: Is there a special subject line or secure way to send docs? Don't want sensitive info in clear email.

Ivy (People Ops): Use subject: "QLE - Foster child placement - add dependent" and upload docs via the secure HR portal link in the Confluence benefits page, or email benefits@redwood.com with attachments if portal isn't accessible. Tara Nguyen: Timing: placement date was Apr 3. If I submit today, when would coverage start?

Ben (Benefits): If the carrier accepts placement as a QLE and you submit within their window (usually 30-60 days), effective date is typically the placement date (Apr 3). Some carriers make it the first of the month following placement—we'll confirm when we file the case.

Tara Nguyen: Will payroll deductions be retroactive? I already saw full premium taken this cycle.

Ivy (People Ops): Payroll will prorate or apply retro credits depending on the effective date and payroll cycle. If retro credit is needed beyond the next pay run, we can request an adjustment. Include your preferred effective date in the email and we'll note it for the carrier.

Tara Nguyen: Any impact on HSA/FSA? Child is under 13 and will have daycare support sometimes.

Ben (Benefits): For HSA: adding a dependent to your medical doesn't change your HSA eligibility unless you enroll in a non-HDHP plan. FSA: dependent care FSA eligibility depends on IRS rules for qualifying persons; foster child placed with you who lives with you generally qualifies. Recommend checking with the FSA admin or a tax advisor for specifics.
