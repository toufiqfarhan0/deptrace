ryan: note Private on-prem uses older kernel (4.x) on several customer appliances. We had a known bug where pagevec batches are small and we hit page-turnover thrash under high KV insert/delete.

chen: another interesting bit — our GPU runtime keeps a small LRU of pinned host pages (for faster H2D), but the reclaim path tries to free them and ends up doing synchronous DMA unpin which blocks the cpu thread. On older kernels that path is less async-friendly.

sean: so two levers: 1) kernel/page reclaim improvements (hugepages+pagevec tuning) 2) runtime change to avoid sync unpin on critical path (deferred unpin, epoch-based reclamation).

aisha: deferred unpin would help latency but could increase peak memory. Private customers with limited GPUs might not accept that. Tradeoff.

maria: agreed. For Dedicated we have more headroom and could enable deferred unpin as an opt-in config flag. For Private/on-prem we may need a safer default.

josh: any evidence of fragmentation causing large contiguous allocation failures? e.g., failure to allocate big KV frames forcing many small allocations and extra bookkeeping?

maria: yes — fragmentation shows up in /proc/buddyinfo dumps. On affected hosts we see many order-0/1 free blocks and very few high-order. Runtime then falls back to multiple small frames which increases metadata and churn.

chen: short-term mitigation we tried: call madvise(DONTNEED) on stale pages to help kernel reclaim faster, and use MADV_FREE where available. That reduced p99 by ~30% in Dedicated.

ryan: for Private, customers with locked-down kernels can't accept madvise fiddling sometimes (policy). Also VPC appliances with custom NICs (SR-IOV) show higher page pin rates due to RDMA-zero-copy flows. That exacerbates pinned-frame reclaim cost.
