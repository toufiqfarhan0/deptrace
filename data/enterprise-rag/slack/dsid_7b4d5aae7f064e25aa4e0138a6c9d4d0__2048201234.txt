Suresh Patel: put emphasis on measurable impact: "% of requests affected" or "# of tenants" if possible. ops can leave estimates with a `~` prefix if unsure. avoid absolutes until confirmed.

Olivia Gomez: here's a candidate status page/update template (one-liner + details):

```
Status: Investigating
Affected: {service} — partial degradation
Impact: Some customers may experience increased latency or errors when calling {endpoint}. ~10% of requests.
Started: {start_time} UTC
Update: Engineers are investigating the root cause. Next update: {eta_next_update} UTC
```

Olivia Gomez: for email/notice to customers (short):
```
Subject: Incident affecting {service} — {short_summary}
Body: We are investigating an issue impacting {service} that may affect {customer_impact}. Our team is working on a fix and will send updates by {eta_next_update}. We apologize for the disruption.
```

Chris Reed: can we add a 'tone guide' block? quick bullets: 
- Prefer plain language over internal acronyms.
- Avoid speculative root causes in customer-facing text.
- Use empathetic language: "We know this impacts your workflow" not "you may lose data".
- Provide clear actionables for customers (eg. retry, fallbacks).

Suresh Patel: for internal updates during the incident, we need a severity-to-language map: 
- P1: explicit "Major outage" allowed in internal comms; customer facing should say "partial/major degradation".
- P2: "Degraded performance".
- P3: "Minor impact".
