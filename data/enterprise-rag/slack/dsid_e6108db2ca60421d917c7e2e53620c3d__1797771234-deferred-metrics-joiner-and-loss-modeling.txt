alex: quick question — thinking out loud about a "deferred metrics joiner" for billing. the idea: ingest raw token/usage events as-is, do minimal enrichment, and delay the heavy join (customer metadata, plan rates, discounts) to a downstream joiner that can run asynchronously. tradeoffs?
mira: sounds promising for ingest throughput. main risk is how much accuracy we can tolerate in near-real-time dashboards and alerts. do we have SLOs for 1h vs 24h billing fidelity?
alex: good point. we currently show provisional usage in the console with a final reconciliation at 24h. proposal: accept ~0.5-2% visible variance for provisional numbers, and mark them as "estimated". final ledger (append-only) will be authoritative.
jon: what about late-arriving customer metadata (e.g., plan changes) that affect prior tokens? if joiner is deferred we need a replay/fixup path. that adds complexity.
tara: replay path is unavoidable. two options: 1) materialize deltas and apply to final ledger (compaction-friendly), or 2) keep full event stream and rerun aggregator at windowed intervals. tradeoff: CPU cost vs storage.
alex: also thinking about loss modeling. when events drop (kafka or ingest backpressure), instead of blocking, we emit an "estimated_token" with source=estimation and confidence score. downstream joiner can fold these into provisional totals and later be replaced by exact events if/when they arrive.
samir: hm. how do we prevent double-charging when estimates are later replaced by real events? need idempotent anchors. propose we tag estimates with synthetic ids and real events with canonical request_id; final dedupe runs by canonical id and a small ttl for synthetic ids.
mira: TTL heuristics important — how long do we keep estimated tokens as candidates for replacement? too short -> risk missing corrections; too long -> complicates reconciler. maybe default 7 days with shorter for high-volume customers (3 days) and longer for flagged enterprise (14 days).
jon: cost angle: deferred joiner lets us run heavy joins on cheaper CPU clusters (batch), but increases complexity for auditability. auditors will want a clear chain from raw event -> transformation -> final crediting. we should log reconciliation runs and diffs.
