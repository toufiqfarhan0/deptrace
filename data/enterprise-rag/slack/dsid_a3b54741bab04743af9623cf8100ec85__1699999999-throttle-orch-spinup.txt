maria: Quick RFC follow-up — since we merged the throttle-orchestration prototype, we need a migration plan for edge proxies. Options on the table: fully central broker, per-host sidecar, or a hybrid (local enforcement + broker leases). Thoughts?

jon: Central broker = strongest global fairness and easier to reason about quota accounting. Downside: added network hop and potential p99 impact. How tolerant are low-latency customers here?

ashok: Sidecar model appeals for Dedicated/Private (no outbound egress). But local-only fairness is noisy and easy to game. We need a fallback mode if broker is unreachable.

sylvie: From a scheduler POV central broker gives us a global backlog view, so we can do weight-adjusted fair scheduling across tenants. Sidecars can approximate with local EWMA, but correctness suffers under heavy skew.

maria: We measured some numbers in the RFC bench: central broker with warm cache ~+2ms p99; cold or cross-region calls spike up to ~20ms. That matters for sub-50ms SLO routes.

jon: Also: abuse detection. Central broker can correlate cross-client patterns quicker. Sidecars detect local retry storms sooner but can't see coordinated abuse.

kdavis: Ops/runbook perspective — rollout complexity is real. Hybrid seems safest: sidecar enforces local caps and requests short-lived leases from the broker for global fairness. If lease acquisition fails, sidecar gracefully degrades to local-only limits.
