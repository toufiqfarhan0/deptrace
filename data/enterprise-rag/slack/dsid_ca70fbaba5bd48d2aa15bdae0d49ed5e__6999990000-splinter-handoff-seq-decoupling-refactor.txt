olivia: quick heads-up — prototyped a 'splinter' handoff refactor that decouples the sequence token from the lease handshake. goal: avoid a single global sequencer and make transfers per-slab/idempotent. sketch in playground, TL;DR: simpler invariants, more messaging but safer correctness boundaries.

matt: curious — what are the main break/risk surfaces? eviction races? stale reads?

olivia: main wins: reduces cross-node contention and avoids a serialized transfer queue. tradeoffs: more cross-node chatter during handoff and need for dedupe + explicit commit/ack. correctness: require transfer id + ack + final commit to flip residency.

srajan: can you paste the proto-ish flow? helps me reason about failure modes.

olivia: sure, proto sketch:

```text
SEND_TRANSFER(prefix, epoch, seq_token, transfer_id)
ACK_RECEIVE(prefix, epoch, seq_token, transfer_id)
COMMIT(prefix, epoch, transfer_id)
RECLAIM_IF_NO_ACK(prefix, epoch, transfer_id)
```

matt: how do we preserve read-after-write during in-flight transfers? what prevents a stale node from serving newer writes?

olivia: seq_token still used locally for version checks. during transfer sender marks prefix as "transferring:epoch=X, transfer_id=Y" and rejects reads < COMMIT epoch. receiver buffers writes until COMMIT. if ACKs never arrive, sender can reclaim after timeout + additional fencing (transfer_id CAS).
