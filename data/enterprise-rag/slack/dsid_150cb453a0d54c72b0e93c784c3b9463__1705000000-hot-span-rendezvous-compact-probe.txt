alex: throwing up a proposal: 'hot-span compaction' — sample hot byte-spans inside large model shards and perform compact-on-write rendezvous copies to reduce long-term fragmentation
rina: summary pls — how is this different from previous compact ideas?
alex: two differences: 1) we track variable-length hot spans (not fixed 64KB buckets) to better follow real token-aligned accesses; 2) remap uses a rendezvous copy pattern that avoids long synchronous pauses by splitting work into bounded micro-copies with a small publish hop
chow: variable-length spans sounds heavier. how do we sample cheaply?
alex: cheap heuristic: maintain a tiny per-shard Bloom+counter sketch keyed by page-offset ranges and recent access counts; when a sketch entry crosses a threshold we grow a span by coalescing adjacent hot pages. sampling is probabilistic and we cap spans per-shard to keep state small
rina: rendezvous copy? explain the publish sequence and safety for live pointers
alex: steps: 1) allocate staging slots, 2) do micro-copy chunks from source->staging (each chunk bounded), 3) once full span copied, publish new mapping in a single atomic descriptor swap, 4) schedule background free of old pages after epoch drain. Readers always consult descriptor+epoch so there is no torn view
chow: micro-copy latency on write path? sounds like writes to cold spans may block
alex: we bound sync work to X ms (configurable). if micro-copy budget exhausted we enqueue remaining chunks to compaction workers and either 1) allow the write to proceed with a stale-path fallback or 2) fall back to a slower serving tier. default behavior is to try small rendezvous copy (<3ms) then enqueue rest and let write continue using old mapping until publish completes
rina: what about staging space? we need spare pages and eviction might reclaim them mid-compaction
alex: staging is ephemeral and tagged. default per-node ephemeral pool 2% memory. compaction holds a soft token while copying; eviction checks token presence and avoids reclaiming slots until compaction finishes or timeout. pool is reclaimed under severe pressure and compaction aborts gracefully (no publish) if it cannot secure space
chow: noisy tenants could force many compactions. fairness?
alex: compaction credits per-tenant throttle retries and queue position. heavy compaction consumes credits which are visible in billing/telemetry. infra can raise limits for dedicated customers
rina: eviction interaction: could we end up oscillating (compact then evict)
alex: sampler output feeds eviction scoring: compacted spans get a boost so they sink lower in eviction rank. also we prefer to compact spans that increase contiguity to reduce eviction scatter
chow: failure modes? interrupted publish or partial copy consistency
alex: publish is atomic; until publish, readers see old mapping. partial copy never published. if a worker dies mid-copy we clean up staging and retry. we keep a tiny compaction journal per-node for visibility and recovery routines
