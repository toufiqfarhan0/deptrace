Ivy L.: quick follow-up from the allocator backpressure sync — saw an odd pattern where tenant A's tiny-allocation storms push pool NVMe into sustained high qd and tenant B latency spikes even though B's usage is low. wondering if our current soft-reserve + global backpressure is unfair.

Omar: yep observed that. global backpressure gates everything when qd crosses threshold; it doesn't respect per-tenant budgets so noisy tenants cause head-of-line blocking.

Vikram: idea: introduce a two-tier backpressure policy — a per-tenant soft-reserve (tokens) and a global emergency floor. normal reclaim uses tenant tokens; when tokens exhausted, tenant gets penalized (throttle + demote-hot) but others keep operating.

Ivy L.: sounds promising. need to think about token refill semantics. naive token-bucket might be too coarse for bursty chat. propose EWMA refill with burst window and separate short-term and long-term budgets.

Omar: sketch:
```
tenant_budget:
  short_tokens: 1000   # drains quickly on bursts
  long_tokens: 10000   # long-term cap
  short_refill_ms: 200
  long_refill_ms: 60000
  penalty_duration_ms: 5000
```
When short_tokens hit 0, throttle allocations from that tenant and route small pages to lazy-demote.

Vikram: +1 but also consider fairness when multiple tenants exhaust short_tokens simultaneously. we should enforce proportional draining: tenant gets share = min(requests, fair_share) based on committed quota or past usage.
