Maya (People Ops): @here Quick update — annual PTO encashment opt-in is live for FY2026. You can elect to cash out up to 5 unused PTO days during the open window (May 1–May 15). Opt-in is per-employee; managers do not need to approve, but payroll will verify balances. Details (taxable, processed on June payroll, see calculator): https://intranet.redwood.com/hr/pto-encash. If you don't opt in, unused days will roll to carryover up to 10 days (carryover rules unchanged).

Samir Patel: Is this available to part-timers and contractors?

Maya (People Ops): Part-timers are prorated based on FTE. Contractors are not eligible for employee PTO encashment — contractors should use the contractor expense process if applicable.

Jules Carter: If I already have approved time off in the rollover window, does that change my encashable balance?

Maya (People Ops): Approved PTO that is already booked/recorded by Apr 30 counts as used. Encashment applies to remaining balance after all recorded leave.

Liam O'Connell: Any tax forms or reporting I should expect?

Claire (Finance): This will appear on your June payslip as regular payroll income and is subject to normal payroll taxes. US employees: not a separate 1099; it’s payroll. Non-US employees: local tax rules apply — we’ll publish a country note in the policy doc.
