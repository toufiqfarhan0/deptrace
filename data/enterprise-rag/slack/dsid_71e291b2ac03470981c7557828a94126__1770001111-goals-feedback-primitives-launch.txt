Sam Brooks: The rubric is narrative-first with clear anchors. It defines 'Insufficient / Meets / Exceeds' with 290-second examples and evidence anchors. Not a scorecard but helps calibration conversations.

Sam Brooks: "Meets (PM): Delivered roadmap commitment; adoption metrics hit target within 2 quarters; stakeholder alignment maintained. Evidence: release notes, adoption dashboard, 1 cross-team doc."
Sam Brooks: "Exceeds (PM): Delivered roadmap commitment early; led cross-team experiment with 15% lift; mentored two PMs on discovery process. Evidence: experiment result, mentoring notes, public retro."

Tina Alvarez: What about non-OKR goals like "professional growth" or DEI work? Those are important but hard to measure.
Sam Brooks: We recommend a dual-field approach: 1) Narrative outcome (what changed) and 2) Proxy metric where possible (number of events run, representation % in hiring panels, retention of mentees). Mark these as 'qualitative KRs' in the template so managers know to treat them contextually.
Melody Chen: Can we see the peer micro-form? Short things get more responses.
Sam Brooks: Peer micro-form (3 fields): 1) Strength (1 line + example link), 2) One area to grow (1 line), 3) One actionable suggestion (1 line). Anonymous by default. We also have a 90-char max per field to keep it sharp.
Rajiv Sharma: Upward feedback? I'd like a safe way to capture manager improvement notes.
Sam Brooks: Added an optional upward-feedback micro-prompt in the flow: "One thing my manager could start/stop/continue to better support my goals (max 200 chars)." Aggregated and anonymized by People Ops, shown as themes to managers after calibration. Participation optional.

Melody Chen: For the manager rubric, any explicit guidance on language to avoid (bias-y phrasing)? Managers sometimes use unhelpful adjectives.
