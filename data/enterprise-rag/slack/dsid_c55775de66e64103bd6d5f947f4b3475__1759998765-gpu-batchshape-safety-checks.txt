Kevin: can we tune queue policy instead of static ceilings? e.g., prefer packing short requests together and reserve a separate queue for long-token requests to avoid kernel thrash.

Maria: that's the idea in the proposal. two levers: 1) per-GPU hard ceiling for max_tokens_per_batch to avoid pathological KV fragmentation, 2) adaptive queue split that forms homogeneous batches when seq length variance > threshold.

Adrian: what ceilings are you proposing per GPU?

Maria: draft ceilings (after calibration):
- T4: 4096 tokens
- A10: 8192 tokens
- L4: 12288 tokens
- A100: 16384 tokens
- H100: 20480 tokens
These are conservative compared to previous unlimited ramp.

Oliver: why is H100 lower than earlier suggestions? it has huge memory.

Maria: H100 has more memory but its kernel selection cost for mixed batches is higher; the expensive fallback kernels punish p99 much more. We prefer a slightly lower ceiling + better queue policy to capture the throughput gains without regressing latency.

Kenneth: how about dynamic ceilings that increase with observed homogeneous batch ratio? e.g., if 90% of batches are homogeneous (seq variance small), raise ceiling by 1.5x temporarily.

Maria: yep. prototype: monitor batch variance metric `batch_seq_cv` and `kv_bytes_est`. rule: if `batch_seq_cv < 0.12` and `kv_bytes_est < device_kv_threshold` for 30s, allow burst to 1.5x ceiling. otherwise decay back to base.

Maya: what about kernel hints from the scheduler? could we pass a preferred kernel tag to reduce fallback churn?

Maria: we can. scheduler can include `preferred_kernel_hint` based on predicted seq shape. two caveats: kernel hinting helps when kernel exists and is compatible; if not compatible the hint is ignored. but it reduced fallback count by ~42% in the lab.

Kevin: any perf regressions after implementing adaptive queue split + per-gpu ceilings?

Maria: ran canary on staging: p99 stabilized or improved across all GPUs except A100 where we saw a small +2% p99 increase for short seqs (investigating). throughput retained ~85-95% of the unconstrained throughput for long tails. overall win.
