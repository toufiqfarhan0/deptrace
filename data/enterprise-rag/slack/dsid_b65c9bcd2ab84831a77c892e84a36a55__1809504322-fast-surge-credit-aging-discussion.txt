Devin: ugh. that matches the fairness problem we saw last quarter. what's your concrete proposal?
Matt: proposal A (simple): add TTL on allocated burst credits so any unused credit decays to 0 after TTL_sec. default TTL = 300s.
Matt: proposal B (gradual): apply exponential aging where available_credit *= exp(-lambda * dt). choose lambda so half-life ~= 60s for "fast" credits.
Priya: TTL is easier to reason about in dashboards. exponential aging smoother but harder to explain to customers.
Iris: from console UX POV TTL is neat — we can show "expires in Xs" per credit chunk. exponential needs animation.
Devin: on fairness: TTL punishes orgs that legitimately accumulate credits during quiet hours for predictable morning spikes. need two pools: short-lived fast credits (for microbursts) + long-lived reserve credits (for planned spikes).
Matt: agree. hybrid: split refill into fast_pool and reserve_pool. fast_pool gets immediate refill up to burst_capacity but ages quickly (TTL or half-life). reserve_pool refills slowly and doesn't age for longer windows. routing policy draws from fast_pool first.
Priya: config sketch: 
```yaml
rate_limit: 100 rps
burst_capacity: 300 tokens
fast_pool: 
  capacity: 100
  ttl_sec: 60  # or use half_life_sec: 60
reserve_pool:
  capacity: 200
  refill_rate_per_min: 10
```
